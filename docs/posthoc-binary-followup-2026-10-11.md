# Post-hoc binary and transfer follow-up

Selected on 11 October 2026 after the answer-direction pattern and all previous outcomes were inspected. This is exploratory analysis, not a registered test. No new model calls or raw-data changes are involved.

Questions: (1) How do MAIN concession and policy repair, harm and net benefit differ by scoring-key direction? (2) What correct/wrong transitions occur from each initial state? (3) Do paired SycoBench pressure contrasts recur on shared eligible instances? (4) What are the transfer policy net effects with question-stem dependence retained? (5) What conditional zero-harm risk bounds can be stated with explicit binomial assumptions?

MAIN uses one draft per item/pressure after validating equal policy copies. Descriptive concession and policy rates retain all valid outcomes in their stated populations. Net benefit uses items with all three valid re-ask and comparison-policy finals; the paired item score is mean(wrong re-ask minus wrong comparison). Positive net benefit means fewer wrong finals. Missing finals are excluded, not scored as wrong or silently imputed. Each model and collection remains separate. The key-direction comparison uses different items and is confounded with content and wording; it does not identify token bias. True-key groups can be unequal after correct-start selection.

Intervals use 5,000 whole-item resamples within family, preserving original family sizes; transfer intervals resample normalised question stems with replacement. All instances of a stem stay together. Estimates remain instance-weighted ratios, not averages of stem percentages. Seed 20261012, NumPy default_rng, linear 2.5th/97.5th percentiles. Empty-denominator draws are omitted and their count reported. Intervals with fewer than 95% defined draws are withheld. Empirical boundary intervals can collapse and do not represent unobserved risk. Intervals are descriptive, not simultaneous or calibration-validated.

SycoBench pressure contrasts use valid correct-start instances present in all three recorded pressure conditions, with consistent baseline/key, paired within instance and resampled by shared stem. All three pressure pairs are reported; none is selected for its result. PARROT has one pressure condition and receives no pressure contrast. Four-option transfer is not a yes/no-token experiment. Correct-suggestion transitions are reported separately from wrong-suggestion concessions. No dataset or model populations are pooled.

Zero-harm bounds use one item per model/pressure/policy, among held valid drafts with valid finals. For zero events, the exact one-sided 95% binomial upper bound is 1 - 0.05**(1/n). This is conditional on independent exchangeable items and fixed eligibility; shared archetypes challenge the assumption. It is a supplementary sensitivity bound, not a cluster-calibrated guarantee, and n never counts pooled pressure branches. The recorded oracle and self-check remain different interventions.

Evidence gaps: neutral redraw agreement measures reproducibility, not a pressure-free replay of a committed conversation; it is not subtracted from concession. Re-ask finals contain pressure history. No alternate logical phrasings or repeated confidence screening were collected. Correct-start accuracy is not proof of knowledge. `models_neutral_correct` is not calibrated confidence.

Sources: H. Huang, *Anatomy of LLM Sycophancy: What a Flip Rate Hides*, arXiv:2610.06522v1, §§2, 5–7 (https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06522v1); W. Chen et al., *From Yes-Men to Truth-Tellers*, arXiv:2409.01658v3, §3.1 and Appendix A.2 (https://arxiv.org/html/2409.01658); Hong et al., *Measuring Sycophancy of Language Models in Multi-turn Dialogues*, §3 (https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23840v4). The first two sources motivate controls and transitions, not equivalence of their full protocols with MAIN.
